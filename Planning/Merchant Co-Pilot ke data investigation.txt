# Part 4 — Why do you need a common model?

Imagine your analytics code says:

```text
calculate_customer_recency()
```

You don't want it to care whether the merchant uses Shopify or WooCommerce.

You want:

```text
Shopify ───────┐
               ↓
           Customer
               ↑
               │
WooCommerce ───┘
```

So your internal database has:

```text
customers
---------
customer_id
store_id
source_platform
source_customer_id
email
created_at
...
```

Then your analytics works on **your fields**, not Shopify/WooCommerce fields.

That's the whole reason for the adapter/common-data-model approach.

# Part 5 — How integration actually works

Let's make it extremely concrete.

Suppose a merchant says:

> "Connect my Shopify store."

Your application does roughly:

```text
Merchant
   ↓
Click "Connect Shopify"
   ↓
Shopify authorization
   ↓
Merchant grants required access
   ↓
Your backend receives authorized connection
   ↓
Identify store
   ↓
Request Shopify data
   ↓
Receive Shopify response
   ↓
Transform Shopify format
   ↓
Validate
   ↓
Save to PostgreSQL
```

For example, Shopify might return an order structure.

Your application transforms it into:

```text
orders

id
store_id
source_platform
source_order_id
customer_id
order_date
order_value
currency
status
...
```

Then your dashboard can query:

```text
SELECT ...
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = ...
```

The merchant never needs to know how Shopify's internal structure works.
